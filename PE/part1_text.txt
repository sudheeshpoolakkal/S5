Power Electronics 
Branch of electrical engineering which deals with the behavior and
operation of high voltage and current devices used to control and
deliver electrical power.
Involves the study of :-
• Power semiconductor devices –their physics, chara, drive requirements and their protection
• Power converter topologies
• Control strategies of the converters
• Digital, analog and microelectronics involved
• Magnetic and capacitive storage elements
• Static and rotating electrical drives
• Analysis of the generated waveforms
• EM and RF interference

POWER ELECTRONICS- Advantages
• High efficiency due to low loss in semiconductor devices.
• High reliability of power electronic converter systems.
• Long life & less maintenance due to absence of any moving parts.
• Fast response as compared to electromechanical  converter systems.
• Small size, less weight, so less floor space, low installation cost.
• Mass production of semiconductor devices result in low cost of the 
converter equipment.


=== PAGE 11 ===
POWER ELECTRONICS- Disadvantages
• Power electronic converters have a tendency to generate harmonics in the 
supply system & in the load circuit.
• AC to DC and AC to AC converters operate at low input power factor. So 
some measures have to be adopted to improve power factor.
• Power electronic controllers have low overload capacity. So these 
controllers must be rated for taking momentary overloads. This may increase 
the cost of power electronic controllers.
• Regeneration of power is difficult in power electronic converter systems.

POWER SEMICONDUCTOR DEVICES- History
•
Mercury-arc rectifiers
•
Robust and compact -Silicon Controlled Rectifier (SCR) replaced
mercury-arc rectifiers
•
Then came bipolar transistors which handle few tens of amperes and
block a few hundred volts - superior in its turn-off capability.
•
Power MOSFET – integration of modern integrated circuit and discrete
power semiconductor manufacturing technologies - higher operating
frequencies upto a few MHz
- high voltage MOSFETs are not
commercially viable
•
Gate-turn-off thyristor (GTO) – was developed as an improvement on
SCR regarding its
turn-off capability-application restricted to inverters
rated above a few hundred kVA
•
Insulated Gate Bipolar Transistor (IGBT) – basically a MOSFET driven
Bipolar device – available upto 4 kV and 4 kA- switching frequency is 25
kHz

=== PAGE 17 ===
POWER SEMICONDUCTOR DEVICES (Contd...)
•
SCR(Silicon Controlled Rectifier), GTO(Gate Turn-Off Thyristor), MCT
(MOSFET Controlled Thyristor) and SIT(Static Induction Transistors) –
require pulse signal for turn-on and once ON the pulse can be removed.
•
BJT (Bipolar Junction Transistor), MOSFET (Metal Oxide Semiconductor
Field Effect Transistor), IGBT (Insulated Gate Bipolar Transistors) and SIT
– require continuous signal for keeping them in the turn-on condition
•
BJT, MOSFET, IGBT and MCT – withstand unipolar voltages
•
Thyristors and GTOs – can withstand bipolar voltages
•
TRIAC and RCT (Reverse Conducting Thyristor) – possess bidirectional
current capability
•
Diodes, SCR, BJT, MOSFET, IGBT, GTO, SIT, MCT – unidirectional
current devices

Power Electronics vs linear Electronics
• In Power Electronics all devices are operated in the switching mode – either
'FULLY-ON' or 'FULLY-OFF' states.
• Power electronics focuses on efficiency and power conversion, often resulting in
lower heat dissipation, and losses
• Linear electronics uses components in their "active" or "linear" region to process
signals, typically for amplification or signal conditioning.
• Linear electronics focuses on signal fidelity with higher efficiency losses and
power consumption.

=== PAGE 25 ===
Power Electronics - benefits
• Electric power is scarce, so prime importance to deliver the power to the load
with minimum losses.
• Advancements in power semiconductor research has helped in achieving this.
The benefits are:-
• High power density power supplies
• Improved efficiency of up to 99% in power conversion
• Noise-sensitive applications such as in medical devices
• Power can be made available in the desired form and level as per
specifications
• Increased clean power generation using power electronic interfaces to connect
the sources to grid
• Wireless power transfer

Power Electronic Devices - features
Power Diodes:-
3 types –
• Line frequency diodes
• Fast recovery diodes
• Schottky diodes
Line frequency diodes:-
• Highest voltage & current – 5 kV & 5 kA
• Excellent over current rating
• Excellent voltage withstand capability


=== PAGE 28 ===
Power Electronic Devices – features (Contd…)
Fast recovery diodes:-
• Available at high power
• Used in association with fast controlled devices
• Used in UPS, traction, induction heating etc.
Schottky diodes:-
• Fastest diodes
• Majority carrier device
• Voltage rating only upto a 100 V
• High current rating

2. Power Transistors 


=== PAGE 30 ===
Power Electronic Devices – features (Contd…)
Power Bipolar transistor:-
• Developed to handle few tens of amperes
& block few hundred volts
• High operating frequency is possible
• Not capable of blocking reverse voltage, so
application is limited
• Superior the turn-off capability
• Preferred in forced commutated converters
• But gain is somewhat less


=== PAGE 31 ===
POWER SEMICONDUCTOR DEVICES (Contd....)
3. MOSFET- Metal Oxide Semiconductor Field Effect


=== PAGE 32 ===
Power Electronic Devices – features (Contd…)
Power MOSFET:-
• High gain
• Ease of paralleling
• High operating frequency
• Voltage fed device
• Majority carrier device
• Needs only a very low current to switch on
• Delivers high current of more than 50 A
• High voltage MOSFETs are not commercially
viable
• Preferred where high switching frequencies are
desired but operating voltages are low


=== PAGE 33 ===
POWER SEMICONDUCTOR DEVICES (Contd....)
4. IGBT- Insulated Gate Bipolar Transistor


=== PAGE 34 ===
Power Electronic Devices – features (Contd…)
IGBT:-
• MOSFET + Bipolar
• Voltage controlled device
• unidirectional
• Available upto 1200 V
• Switching frequency is approx. 25 kHz
• Ease of connection
• Requires only a small voltage to maintain conduction
through the device
• Does not require protection circuits
• Can be connected in parallel without difficulty
• Replaced BJT in almost all applications


=== PAGE 35 ===
THYRISTOR FAMILY– features
SCR:-
• Most popular in the thyristor family
• Unidirectional device
• Turned-on by the application of a gate signal
• Voltage rating of forward and reverse blocking are
almost equal
• 2 types – converter grade (phase controlled
thyristor) and inverter grade
• Converter grade – main element of PE- available at voltage
ratings above 5 kV and 5 kA
• Inverter grade – called fast grade SCRs


=== PAGE 36 ===
THYRISTOR FAMILY– features (Contd…)
TRIAC:-
• TRIODE for alternating current(TRIode + AC)
• Bidirectional
• 3 terminal device
• Controls the flow of current
• Similar to 2 thyristors connected back to back
• Regarded as DIAC with an extra gate terminal

=== PAGE 38 ===
THYRISTOR FAMILY– features (Contd…)
TRIAC:- (Contd...)
• Used in applications which does not require very high power
• Used in light dimmers, small electric fans etc.
• Facilitates switching operation in function in AC electrical components
& systems.
• Not used in high power applications because it causes EMI.


=== PAGE 39 ===
THYRISTOR FAMILY– features (Contd…)
DIAC:-
• DIODE for alternating current(DIode + AC)
• Bidirectional depending on the voltage across its
terminals
• 2 terminal device
• Conducts when the voltage across the
terminals is greater than the breakdown voltage
• Similar to 2 diodes connected back to back
• Used for triggering of TRIACs, speed control of
universal motors


=== PAGE 40 ===
THYRISTOR FAMILY– features (Contd…)
GTO:-
• High cost
• High switching speed than SCR
• Can be turned-on by a short pulse of gate signal and
turned-off by a reverse gate signal.
• Used in inverters rated above a few 100 kVA
