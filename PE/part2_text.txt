=== PAGE 4 ===
THYRISTORS (Contd…)
• Has a PNPN structure
• Can be viewed as a PNP and NPN transistor connected back-to-back
• THYRISTOR               THYRatron + transISTOR
• Is a device similar to a transistor and has characteristics similar to a 
thyratron tube
• Once the device is ON it acts like a rectifier and so it is called as SCR. 
• It has common properties of diodes, resistors and transistors.
• Thyristor is a unidirectional device and conducts current in only one 
direction. 
• It can be used only for switching and cannot be used for amplification.


=== PAGE 5 ===
THYRISTORS -Principle
• 4 layered PNPN semiconductor device, having 3 
junctions J1, J2, J3 and 3 terminals-Anode (A), 
Cathode (K) and Gate (G).
• Main terminals – Anode and Cathode
• Control terminal – Gate
• Gate terminal is kept near the Cathode
• Outer p-region is the Anode
• Outer n-region is the Cathode
• Inner p-region is the Gate
• Gate terminal controls the device
• Voltage rating of 10 kV and rms current rating of 
3 kA are available

TWO TRANSISTOR MODEL
•
SCR or thyristor is a three terminal semiconductor device having P-
N-P-N structure.
•
Two transistor analogy of SCR is a method of representing SCR in
the form of two transistor model.
•
The basic operating principle of SCR can realized by using the two
transistor model.
•
This represents SCR as the combination of PNP and NPN transistor.
•
Anode of the SCR is connected to the emitter of the PNP transistor
•
Cathode of the SCR is connected to the emitter of NPN transistor
•
Both transistors are connected in common base configuration


=== PAGE 9 ===
THYRISTORS – Static V-I characteristics
• 3 modes of operation of a thyristor –
- reverse blocking mode
- forward blocking mode (off-state)
- forward conduction mode (on-state)
• Anode and cathode are connected to the main source E through the
load
• Gate and cathode is connected to a source Es .
• Es provides a +ve gate current from gate to cathode
• Switching the SCR from forward blocking state (OFF – state) to
forward conduction state (ON – state) is known as turning-ON
process of SCR. This is also called as triggering.
• The criteria for triggering the SCR depends on the several factors like
supply voltage, gate current, temperature, etc.

Static V-I characteristics (Contd….)
Reverse blocking mode:-
• Also called off-state of the thyristor
• When cathode is +ve wrt anode and
switch S open, the thyristor is
reverse biased.
• Junction J2 is forward biased and J1
and J3 are reverse biased.
• Device is now similar to two
diodes connected in series across a
reverse voltage
• Small leakage current (of the order of
a few milliamps or microamps depending
on the rating of the SCR) flows.


=== PAGE 12 ===
Static V-I characteristics (Contd….)
Reverse blocking mode:- (Contd…)
• When the reverse voltage is increased an
avalanche occurs at J1 and J3 at a critical
breakdown value.
• This breakdown value is called reverse
breakdown voltage, VBR.
• Now the reverse current increases rapidly
• Large current give rise to more losses in the
SCR
• May damage the thyristor as the junction
temperature may exceed its permissible
value.


=== PAGE 13 ===
Static V-I characteristics (Contd….)
Reverse blocking mode:- (Contd…)
• So the maximum working reverse voltage
should not exceed VBR.
• When the applied reverse voltage is less
than VBR the device offers high impedance
in the reverse direction.
• SCR in this mode is treated as an open
switch


=== PAGE 14 ===
Static V-I characteristics (Contd….)
Forward blocking mode:-
• When anode is +ve wrt cathode and switch S
open, the thyristor is forward biased.
• Junction J2 is reverse biased and J1 and J3 are
forward biased.
• Small current flows, known as the forward
leakage current.


=== PAGE 15 ===
Static V-I characteristics (Contd….)
Forward blocking mode:- (Contd...)
• When the forward voltage is increased the
reverse biased junction J2 will have an avalanche
breakdown occurs at a critical breakdown value.
• This
critical
value
is
known
as
forward
breakover voltage, VBO.
• When forward voltage is less VBO than SCR offer

• SCR in this mode is treated as an open switch


=== PAGE 16 ===
Static V-I characteristics (Contd….)
Forward Conduction mode:-
• The SCR conducts current from anode to cathode
with a very small voltage drop across it.
• Thyristor is moved from forward blocking to
forward conduction mode by turning it ON with
a voltage greater than the
forward breakover
voltage across the SCR or by applying a gate
pulse across gate and cathode.
• In this mode the SCR is in the on-state
• Behaves like a closed switch


=== PAGE 17 ===
Static V-I characteristics (Contd….)
Forward Conduction mode:- (Contd....)
• On-state voltage drop is of the order of 1 to 2 volt (it
depends on the rating of the SCR).
• On-state voltage drop increases slightly with the increase
in anode current.
• Anode current is limited by the load impedance alone in
this mode because the voltage drop across SCR is very
small
• This small voltage drop is due to the ohmic drop in the
four layers.
• VBO and VBR are nearly same , but VBR slightly greater
than VBO.
• VBO is taken as the final voltage rating of the device for
design purpose

Latching current (Il ) :- defined as the minimum value of anode current
which it must attain during turn-on process to maintain conduction
even if the gate signal is removed.
•
Once the thyristor starts conducting gate loses its control.
•
The thyristor can be turned-off only if the anode current (forward
current) falls below a low-level current called the holding current.
Holding current (Ih ):- defined as the minimum value of anode current
below which it must fall for turning-off the thyristor.

SWITCHING CHARACTERISTICS
• Static & switching chara of thyristors are required for the reliable
design of converters
• Switching chara – also known as dynamic or transient chara
• During turn-on and turn-off, a thyristor is subjected to different
voltages across it and different current through it.
• Switching chara – variation of voltage across a thyristor and current
through it plotted against time


=== PAGE 22 ===
SWITCHING CHARACTERISTICS-during turn-on
• A forward biased thyristor is turned on by applying a +ve voltage
between gate and cathode.
• There is a transition time from forward off-state to forward on-state,
called turn-on time.
• Turn-on time:- defined as the time during which it changes form
forward blocking to final on-state.
• Total turn-on time (Ton)– 3 intervals - delay time (td), rise time (tr), and
spread time (tp)


=== PAGE 23 ===
SWITCHING CHARACTERISTICS
-during turn-on & turn-off


=== PAGE 24 ===
SWITCHING CHARACTERISTICS-during turn-on
Delay time (td) :-
• time measured from the instant at which gate
current reaches 0.9Ig to the instant at which
anode current reaches 0.1 Ia. (time from 0.9Ig to
0.1 Ia).
• Ig & Ia are the final values of gate current &
anode current.
• time during which anode voltage falls from Va to
0.9Va, where Va = initial value of anode voltage
• Time during which the anode current rises from
the forward leakage current to 0.1 Ia.


=== PAGE 25 ===
SWITCHING CHARACTERISTICS-during turn-on
Delay time (td) :- (Contd...)
• In the forward blocking state, the anode voltage is OA & anode current is
small leakage current.
• Turn-on process is indicated by a rise in the anode current from the small
leakage current.
• At the same time there is a drop in the anode-cathode voltage from the
forward blocking voltage.


=== PAGE 26 ===
SWITCHING CHARACTERISTICS-during turn-on
Delay time (td) :- (Contd...)
• When a gate signal is applied a gate
current starts to flow from gate to cathode.
• Due to the p-layer, the gate current has a
non-uniform distribution
• Its value is higher near the gate , decreases
rapidly as the distance from the gate
increases
• During delay time anode current flows in a
narrow region near the gate.
• i.e., gate current density is highest near the
gate.


=== PAGE 27 ===
SWITCHING CHARACTERISTICS-during turn-on
Rise time (tr) :-
• time taken by the anode current to rise from 0.1Ia to 0.9Ia
• Time required by the forward blocking off-state voltage to fall from 0.9
to 0.1 of its initial value
• Inversely proportional to magnitude of gate current
• Can be reduced if high and steep current pulses are applied to the gate
• Nature of anode circuit mainly determines the rise time.


=== PAGE 28 ===
SWITCHING CHARACTERISTICS-during turn-on
Spread time (tp) :- (Contd...)
• time taken by the anode current to rise from 0.9Ia to Ia
• Time required by the forward blocking voltage to fall from 0.1 of its
initial value to the on-state drop which is around 1 to 1.5V
• During tp the conduction spreads over the entire cross-section of the
cathode of SCR
• Spreading interval depends on the area of the cathode and gate structure
• After tp anode current reaches steady state value
• Also the voltage drop across SCR is equal to the on-state voltage drop.


=== PAGE 29 ===
SWITCHING CHARACTERISTICS-during turn-on
• Total turn-on time = delay time + rise time + spread time
• Total turn-on time depends on anode circuit parameters and waveshape
of the gate signal.
• Rise time is typically of the order of 1 to 4 μs
• During turn-on SCR is considered as a charge controlled device
• Certain amount of charge must be injected into the gate region for the
conduction to begin.
• So higher the gate current lesser will be the time to inject this charge
• Turn-on time can be reduced by using gate currents of higher values
• Magnitude of gate current is usually 3 to 5 times the minimum gate
current required to trigger an SCR.

=== PAGE 31 ===
Turn off characteristics
• The turn off time tq of a thyristor is defined as the time 
between the instant anode current become zero and the 
instant SCR regains forward blocking capability.  (3 to 100μs)
• tq=trr+tgr.
trr - Reverse recovery time  
tgr - gate recovery time  
• The reason for the reversal of anode current after t1 is due 
to the presence of carriers stored in the 4 layers.


=== PAGE 32 ===
• The reverse voltage surge appears across the thyristor
terminals may damage it.
• This is avoided by using protective RC elements across SCR.
• At instance t3, when reverse recovery current has fallen to 
nearly zero value, end junctions J1 and J3 recover and SCR is 
able to block the reverse voltage
• J2 still has trapped charges. Therefore the thyristor is not 
able to block forward voltage at t3.
• Trapped charges gets decayed only by recombination.
• Recombination is possible by maintaining a reverse voltage 
(0 to -50V) across SCR


=== PAGE 33 ===
• The time for the recombination of charges between t3and 
t4 is called gate recovery time
• At t4, J2 recovers, forward voltage can be reapplied between 
anode and cathode
• The turn off time provided to SCR by practical circuit is 
called circuit turn off time, tc
• Tc must be greater than tq for reliable turn off, otherwise 
commutation failure (undesired turn on) may happen
• Converter grade SCR : tq - 50 to 100μs
• Inverter grade SCR : tq - 3 to 50μs

THYRISTOR RATINGS
• Different ratings are:- voltage, current, power, dv/dt, di/dt, turn-on 
time, turn-off time etc.
• Representation of subscripts:-
• First subscript indicate the direction or state: D- forward blocking 
region with gate circuit open, T-on-state, R-reverse, F-forward
• Second subscript denotes the operating values: W- working value, R-
repetitive value, S- surge or non-repetitive value, T- trigger
• Third subscript: M- maximum value
• G- gate ratings, A –stands for anode, AV - stands for average

• maximum rate of rise of the anode voltage that will not trigger the device

=== PAGE 42 ===
CURRENT RATINGS
• A thyristor – made up of semiconductor material – so thermal capacity 
is small 
• junction temperature is dependent on the current handled by a thyristor
• even for short over currents the junction temperature may exceed rated 
value and the thyristor may get damaged
• Proper selection of the current rating is essential for a long working life 
of the device.


=== PAGE 43 ===
CURRENT RATINGS
(i) ITAV -Average on-state current:
- it is the allowable average current that can be applied safely such that
maximum junction temperature and rms current limit are not exceeded.
- the forward voltage drop across SCR during conduction is low and
the power loss in a thyristor mainly depends on the average on-state current.
- for the junction temperature to remain within the limits, SCR must be
rated at a value lower than ITAV .
- repetitive in nature.
- this current determines the application of the device
- Dependent on maximum junction temperature
- With better cooling this rating can be improved


=== PAGE 44 ===
CURRENT RATINGS
(ii) Irms – rms current:
- heating of resistive elements of a thyristor depends on forward 
rms current.
- this rating is used as the upper limit of anode current rating of 
the thyristor. 
- Repetitive in nature.
- Dependent on maximum junction temperature
- With better cooling this rating can be improved


=== PAGE 45 ===
CURRENT RATINGS
(iii) Surge current rating:
- Maximum possible non repetitive or surge current which the device can
withstand.
- When a thyristor is working below its repetitive voltage and current rating,
its permissible junction temperature is never exceeded.
- Due to faults or short circuits, the thyristor may be subjected to abnormal
operating conditions.
- Higher currents caused by non repetitive faults or short circuits should
occur once in a while during the life span of the thyristor to prevent its
degradation.
- Surge currents are sine waves with a frequency of 50 or 60 Hz, depending
on the supply frequency
-This rating specified in terms of the number of surge cycles with
corresponding surge current peak.
- this rating is inversely proportional to the duration of the surge


=== PAGE 46 ===
CURRENT RATINGS
(vi) Other ratings:
- Latching & holding currents
- Turn-on & turn-off times
- Gate circuit voltage, current and power ratings


=== PAGE 47 ===
CURRENT RATINGS
(v) di/dt rating:
- indicates the maximum rate of rise of current from anode to cathode
- when a thyristor is turned on conduction starts near the gate and this
small area of conduction spreads to the whole area of the junction.
- If the rate of rise of anode current (di/dt) is large as compared to
spread velocity of carriers across the cathode junction local hot spots will be
formed near the gate due to high current density.
- This causes the junction temperature to rise above safe value and SCR
will be damaged permanently.
- Therefore a limit on the value of di/dt at turn-on is specified.
- typical values of di/dt are 20 to 500 A/μsec.


=== PAGE 48 ===
Thyristor protection
• For reliable operation of SCR, it should be operated within 
the specific ratings.
• SCRs are very delicate devices and so they must be 
protected against abnormal operating conditions.
• Various protection of SCR are
• di/dt protection
• dv/dt protection
• Over voltage protection
• Over current protection
• Gate protection


=== PAGE 49 ===
di/dt protection
• If di/dt is large as compared to the spread velocity of 
carriers, local hot spots will be formed near the gate on 
account of high current density
• localised heating may destroy SCR
• The value of di/dt can be maintained below specified limit 
by using small inductor called di/dt inductor
• Typical  di/dt limit value of SCR – 20-500 A/μs
• Gate current can be applied near to specified maximum 
value to avoid local  spot heating


=== PAGE 50 ===
dv/dt protection
• During forward blocking mode, J2 has the characteristics of 
a capacitor
• Charging current, iC=C.dv/dt. ie, when dv/dt is high, iC is 
high.
• This high current may turn ON SCR even when gate current 
is zero. This is called as dv/dt turn ON or false turn ON of 
SCR.
• To protect the thyristor against false turn ON or against high 
dv/dt a "Snubber Circuit" is used.


=== PAGE 51 ===
Snubber circuit
• The snubber Circuit is a series combination of resistor 'R' and 
capacitor 'C'.
• They are connected across the thyristor to be protected.
• The capacitor 'C' is used to limit the dv/dt
across the SCR.
• The resistor 'R' is used to limit high 
discharging current through the SCR.
• When switch S is closed, the capacitor 
'C' behaves as a short-circuit.
• Therefore voltage across SCR is zero.
• As time increases, voltage across 'C' increases at a slow rate.
• Therefore dv/dt across 'C' and SCR is less than maximum dv/dt
rating of the device.
• The capacitor charges to full voltage Vs; after which the gate is 
triggered, and SCR is turned ON and  current flows through SCR.
